## Identifying Effective Strategies for Learning: 

##### 1.) Choose a moment in your educational career (it could be an assignment or a full course) where learning went really well. What strategies did you employ that worked particularly well (e.g., working with others, trying work on your own before asking a friend, going to office hours)?
One time a friend and I did our homework in a cafe, and while that seems like an ordinary method to studying, I really did like how my friend and I were strong in different areas of the homework. As such, we were able to help each other and build off of the other's ideas, which I believe contributed to a productive and extensive learning environment.

##### 2.) Similar to (1), which sorts of strategies have led to either less effective learning or less enjoyment of the learning experience. Feel free to describe a few examples of what doesn’t work for you.
A group where everyone does their own thing and only reconvene in the end is not effective whatsoever for me. I end up not learning the other person's methods and processes, and the lack of collaboration only contributes to an individual project with no learning involved since people divided the work to do what they think would be easiest for them. Additionally, learning solely through the course's materials never actually helped me, I found that outside materials and references contributed in my learning. As such, I think it's vital I have outside resources, maybe like textbooks or educational videos, to help me along with the journey.

##### 3.) Particular methods of using AI (e.g., prompting the AI to provide answers to questions and thoughtlessly copying the answers) are unlikely to achieve the learning goals articulated previously. Do you agree with this framing? How are you thinking about AI tools with respect to this course?

I wholeheartedly agree with this framing. I actively avoid using AI tools in providing answers, even if it provides the process in achieving the results, because I know that I haven't actually applied my brain in any way. I believe that proactively completing the assignments myself, even if it's wrong, builds memory and pivots that will help me for future assignments and projects. I think that AI tools can still be helpful in other ways with respect to this course. Aside from writing code or answering questions, AI can help you understand the problem itself if that's what you were struggling with or act as a database searcher and refiner to help find resources for the course. 

##### 4.) What strategies will you use in this course to be successful? With respect to AI, what principles or strategies will you use during this course.

I will be proactive in my learning. That means attending OHs (even if I think I barely need them) and ensuring that I understand a topic thoroughly before progressing. Furthermore, I'm still in the process of finding a helpful textbook or similar written material to follow along with, of which I believe would be a very helpful strategy to improve my learning. As for AI usage, I don't really plan on using it at all unless I really don't understand something that online materials or CAs can't help with. 

##### 5.) What do you think of some of the proposed activities for the oral quizzes? Are these activities ones that would give you helpful feedback as to how you are performing with respect to the course material? Would you add or subtract any of the proposed activities?

I think these are great ways to test our progress in this course and see if I'm super behind on everything. I have no problem with this but compared to the other options I'm not sure how this will demonstrate my learning (especially because I'm pretty bad explaining things or teaching someone / demonstrating my process) but I'm willing to try it out!

Similar to the doc that provides resources for other languages, I think providing some resources for the actual material we learn in class rather than just the languages would be very helpful.
